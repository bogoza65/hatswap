Privacy policy

Last updated 30 October 2026 · Applies to the Hatswap Chrome extension

The short version

Hatswap stores everything on your own computer and sends nothing anywhere. It has no servers, no accounts and no analytics. The extension makes no network requests at all.

What Hatswap stores

  • Saved logins: for each account you save, a copy of that website's cookies (including login cookies) and its localStorage and sessionStorage, plus the page you were last on. These are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key derived from your master password (PBKDF2, 600,000 iterations) and kept in Chrome's local extension storage on your device.
  • Account names, client groups, colours and timestamps: stored unencrypted, so the toolbar badge can show the active account while Hatswap is locked.
  • Your settings: auto-lock time, auto-save, what happens after a switch.
  • A learning record per account of which login cookies normally stay the same, so Hatswap can notice when you've signed in to a different account by hand. This contains cookie names, not values.

Your master password itself is never stored. While Hatswap is unlocked, the derived key is held in memory only and is discarded when Hatswap locks or Chrome quits.

What Hatswap never does

  • Send your data, or anything derived from it, to Hatswap's developer or any third party.
  • Collect usage statistics, crash reports or analytics.
  • Read websites you haven't saved an account on. Access is requested one website at a time, when you first save an account there, and is removed when you delete the last account for that site.
  • Load remote code.

Permissions, and why

  • cookies and scripting: to read and restore each account's session on the sites you've chosen.
  • storage: to keep your encrypted accounts and settings on your device.
  • tabs and activeTab: to know which site the current tab is on and reload it after a switch.
  • Per-site access: granted by you, per website, when you save an account there.

Deleting your data

Deleting an account in Hatswap removes its saved session. Settings → Erase All Data removes everything Hatswap stored, including the master password, and gives back the site access you granted. Uninstalling the extension does the same.

Verifying this

The source code is public at github.com/bogoza65/hatswap. Anyone can confirm that the extension contains no network calls.
